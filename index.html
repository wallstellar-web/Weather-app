How the prediction is made

Rainwatch pulls the same location from seven global weather models: ECMWF (Europe), UK Met Office, DWD ICON (Germany), Météo-France, NOAA GFS (USA), GEM (Canada) and JMA (Japan). For places in India it adds the official IMD district nowcast when connected. Each hour, a model "votes" rain if it predicts at least 0.2 mm. Votes are weighted: ECMWF carries the most because it scores highest in published global verification, then UKMO, ICON and the rest.

The rain chance blends that weighted vote (70%) with the probability from Open-Meteo's best-match blend (30%). In India, an active IMD nowcast counts for about 40% of the rain chance during its 3-hour window, because it is issued by forecasters watching Doppler radar. Rain timing within the hour comes from 15-minute data. Your Ground truth reports raise or lower each model's weight for your area.

Indian sources · IMD

Checking

Official India Meteorological Department district nowcasts (rain, thunderstorm and lightning for the next 3 hours), 5-day district warnings and season rainfall. For places in India they feed the rain chance and can trigger alerts on their own.

Connect IMD data

IMD serves its API only to approved, IP-whitelisted servers. To connect: request API access from IMD's nodal officer (contacts are on mausam.imd.gov.in under APIs), run the Rainwatch IMD relay on the approved server, then enter the relay address here.

India's own forecast models, NCMRWF's NCUM and IMD's GFS T1534, are not published as point forecasts that apps can read. Rainwatch already includes the models they are built on: NCUM uses the UK Met Office Unified Model, and IMD's GFS comes from NOAA's GFS.
